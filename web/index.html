What is this?

Michael Ruhlman's Ratio claims that the base ingredient ratios determine what kind of baked good you get. This project tests that at scale: 2,231,142 recipes are reduced to five structural parts and scored against the independent class labels Food.com attached to them. The ratios are the features, the tags are the labels, and the two are never allowed to peek at each other.

The result is a partial yes. The classes are described in ratio space, not predicted from it: their centroids sit where the book says they should (cake leans rich, bread leans flour, cookies sit in the middle), but the classes overlap almost completely. Baking looks like a continuum, and the Food.com tags are fuzzy labels painted on it. Nothing here is clustered or compressed: the geometry you see is the geometry that was computed.

The Aid

Every recipe is a point on the simplex of its five parts. A partition groups those parts: two groups make a 1-D axis, three a triangle, four a tetrahedron. The divider bar above the plot lets you drag parts between compartments and watch the space fold. Search highlights where your query lives; click a point for its ratio card, its nearest book archetype, and its class neighbourhood.

Where the data comes from

Three independent corpora meet at exactly one place: the numeric Food.com id at the end of each RecipeNLG link. The join is the only point of contact between the ingredients and the labels.

RecipeNLG

2.23 M recipes · ~2.3 GB

Raw ingredient text and source links. Bien et al. 2020. Supplies the recipe lines that get parsed.

recipenlg.cs.put.poznan.pl

Food.com

267,782 recipes

Tags, the independent class labels, plus per-recipe nutrition. shuyangli94 on Kaggle, 2019.

kaggle.com/shuyangli94

USDA FoodData Central

7,793 foods · SR Legacy 2018-04

The final Standard Reference release. Per-100 g proximates decompose each ingredient head into structural parts.

fdc.nal.usda.gov

Stage 1 is verified byte-identical across runs, and the dataset is committed, so the Aid rebuilds without the raw download. Everything is plain Python and vanilla JavaScript, with no framework and no server.
